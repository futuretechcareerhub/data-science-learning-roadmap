How to Perform Root Cause Analysis with Data Science: Find the Reasons Behind Business Problems

Move beyond surface symptoms, investigate data systematically, and identify evidence-backed reasons behind business performance changes.

Declining sales, rising complaints, falling conversions or unexpected costs often trigger quick assumptions. Root cause analysis (RCA) helps teams investigate what is behind the visible problem rather than treating symptoms alone. Data science supports RCA through business understanding, data checks, segmentation, analytical reasoning and hypothesis validation.

Remember: Patterns are clues, not automatic proof of causation. Check alternative explanations and the strength of evidence before recommending action.

1. Define the business problem clearly

Specify the metric, change, comparison period and affected area. Instead of “sales are down,” ask: “Revenue fell 12% over the last four weeks versus the previous four weeks. Is the change explained by traffic, conversion, order value, channel, product or region?”

2. Separate symptoms from hypotheses

The KPI decline is a symptom. Possible explanations may include traffic mix, pricing, stock availability, campaign changes, website experience, seasonality, customer mix or faulty tracking. Write down plausible hypotheses before exploring so you do not overfit your story to the first chart.

3. Validate the data first

  • Inspect missing values, duplicates and unexpected records.
  • Confirm metric definitions and date/time handling.
  • Check data refresh delays and tracking changes.
  • Use comparable time periods and consistent filters.
  • Review whether source systems or reporting pipelines changed.

4. Decompose the metric into drivers

For example, Revenue = Orders × Average Order Value, and Orders = Sessions × Conversion Rate. If sessions are stable but conversion falls, traffic volume may not explain the full revenue decline. Breaking a KPI into components narrows the search.

5. Segment to localize the issue

Compare channels, product categories, regions, devices, customer cohorts and time periods. Overall averages can hide a segment-specific problem or shift because the customer mix changed. Check sample sizes and whether segment differences are meaningful.

6. Investigate with Python and Pandas

Illustrative dataset: weekly metrics by channel, with a previous and latest period.

import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv("weekly_channel_metrics.csv")
df["conversion_rate"] = df["orders"] / df["sessions"]

latest = df[df["period"] == "latest"]
previous = df[df["period"] == "previous"]

comparison = latest.merge(
    previous, on="channel", suffixes=("_latest", "_previous")
)
comparison["conversion_change_pp"] = (
    comparison["conversion_rate_latest"]
    - comparison["conversion_rate_previous"]
)
comparison["sessions_change_pct"] = (
    comparison["sessions_latest"] / comparison["sessions_previous"] - 1
) * 100

print(comparison.sort_values("conversion_change_pp"))

Use this output to identify segments worth investigating. Validate data definitions and consider uncertainty before treating differences as meaningful.

7. Test alternative explanations

  • Did channel or customer mix shift?
  • Were pricing, promotions or product availability changed?
  • Was a site/app update released?
  • Could seasonality or a holiday influence the comparison?
  • Is tracking incomplete or inconsistent?

Choose validation appropriate to the question—statistical comparisons, confidence intervals, time-series checks, experiments, technical logs or domain review. Correlation and timing alone do not prove cause.

8. Translate findings into actions

For each supported insight, document what changed, where, supporting evidence, confidence, limitations, recommended action, owner and success metric. Prioritize by potential impact, evidence strength, effort and risk. Where evidence is weak, propose a targeted investigation or experiment.

Illustrative business case

Suppose revenue drops 12%, sessions stay broadly stable, and segmentation shows mobile conversion fell while desktop conversion did not. Investigating a recent release might reveal a checkout issue affecting some mobile users. Validate with logs or a controlled fix, then monitor conversion and revenue. This is a hypothetical example of the workflow, not a real company finding.

Root cause analysis checklist

  • ☐ Define KPI, baseline and timeframe.
  • ☐ Validate data quality and measurement.
  • ☐ List candidate explanations.
  • ☐ Decompose metric drivers.
  • ☐ Segment relevant groups.
  • ☐ Check alternatives and uncertainty.
  • ☐ Distinguish correlation from causation.
  • ☐ Document recommendations, owners and metrics.

Conclusion

Root cause analysis with data science combines structured problem framing, reliable data, metric decomposition, segmentation and hypothesis testing. The goal is not just to explain a chart but to produce a defensible recommendation that helps a business decide what to do next.
